AG Cars: A Theoretical Framework for Electromagnetically Suspended Transportation Systems

Abstract

This paper proposes a theoretical transportation system known as AG Cars (Artificial Gravity Cars). Unlike traditional wheel-based vehicles or track-bound magnetic levitation systems, AG Cars are envisioned as independently suspended transportation vehicles capable of hovering above the ground through electromagnetic suspension while utilizing air propulsion systems for directional movement.

Once a vehicle is suspended above the ground, wheel traction is no longer necessary; propulsion can instead be achieved through controlled air displacement while stabilization is maintained through electromagnetic suspension systems.

Existing technologies such as maglev trains, hovercraft systems, drone stabilization, superconducting magnetic suspension, and ducted-fan propulsion provide partial evidence that the individual components required for AG Cars already exist in fragmented forms.

1. Introduction

Modern transportation systems rely almost entirely on friction-based mechanics. Cars use wheels to transfer force through contact with roads, creating rolling resistance, mechanical wear, energy loss, tire degradation, and infrastructure erosion.

The AG Car theory proposes that transportation efficiency may significantly increase if physical contact between vehicle and roadway is eliminated entirely.

Friction exists because of contact. Remove contact, and transportation becomes limited primarily by air resistance rather than surface resistance.

2. Core Theory of AG Cars

  • Suspension
  • Stabilization
  • Propulsion

Traditional cars combine all three functions into wheel mechanics. AG Cars distribute these functions across separate engineering systems.

3. Suspension Theory

The proposed theory suggests dynamically controlled electromagnetic fields between the roadway and the vehicle.

  • Electromagnetic attraction
  • Electromagnetic repulsion
  • Dynamically balanced field control

Instead of relying on wheel contact, the vehicle remains suspended approximately 1–2 meters above the surface.

4. Propulsion Theory

Once suspended, wheel traction becomes unnecessary. AG Cars theoretically use:

  • Ducted fans
  • Compressed air propulsion
  • Electric turbine systems
  • Vector thrust systems

The propulsion system functions by pushing air backward, generating forward thrust through Newton’s Third Law.

5. Stability Problem

The greatest engineering challenge is stabilization. A suspended vehicle naturally becomes unstable because electromagnetic fields fluctuate and air turbulence affects positioning.

AG Cars would likely require:

  • AI-assisted balancing
  • Predictive stabilization systems
  • Continuous electromagnetic correction
  • Real-time environmental adaptation

6. Existing Technologies

Maglev Trains

Demonstrate large-scale electromagnetic suspension.

Hovercraft

Demonstrate transportation without direct wheel friction.

Drones & eVTOL

Demonstrate autonomous stabilization and distributed propulsion.

7. Smart Infrastructure

Future smart roads may contain:

  • Embedded electromagnetic conductors
  • Wireless energy transfer systems
  • AI-assisted traffic coordination
  • Stabilization assistance fields

8. Energy Challenges

The largest practical limitation remains energy efficiency. Continuous suspension and propulsion require significant electrical output.

9. Conclusion

AG Cars represent a theoretical evolution of transportation systems beyond wheel-based mechanics. By combining electromagnetic suspension, AI stabilization, thrust propulsion, and autonomous coordination, AG Cars may represent one of the most significant future transformations in transportation engineering.
